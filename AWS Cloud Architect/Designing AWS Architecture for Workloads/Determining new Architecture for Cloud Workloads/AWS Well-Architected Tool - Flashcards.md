#flashcards/aws/workloads/well-architected-tool

Flashcards for [[AWS Well-Architected Tool]].

What is the AWS Well-Architected Tool?
?
A service that provides a consistent process for measuring your architecture against AWS best practices, documenting decisions and recommending improvements.

What are the steps of a Well-Architected Tool review?
?
Identify the workload, answer a series of questions about its architecture, then the tool reviews the answers against the framework's pillars.

What outputs does the Well-Architected Tool provide after a review?
?
Videos and documentation on AWS best practices, a report summarizing the workload review, and a dashboard of workload reviews across the organization.

Which pillars does the AWS Well-Architected Framework use?
?
Operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimization, and sustainability.

How many pillars does the Well-Architected Framework currently have, and which one was added most recently?
?
Six. Sustainability was added in 2021, after the original five.

How did Cox Automotive conduct Well-Architected reviews before adopting the tool?
?
Manually, working through the framework questions with printed PDFs and spreadsheets.

What did Cox Automotive achieve by using the Well-Architected Tool?
?
Executive buy-in for security and resilience investment, a data-driven culture aligning 350 engineering teams, and over $700,000 a year in savings compared with on-prem.

Beyond technical improvements, what does the Cox case study show the Well-Architected Tool is useful for?
?
Communicating risk and value to leadership to secure resources and drive organizational change.

Which two modernization strategies follow from typical Well-Architected Tool recommendations?
?
Enhancing data processing capabilities, and improving application architecture.

Which pillars' recommendations most often lead to architecture modifications, according to the lecture?
?
Reliability and performance efficiency.

Why should Well-Architected reviews be repeated rather than done once?
?
Workloads and risks change over time, so continuous reviews keep findings current and show improvement.
